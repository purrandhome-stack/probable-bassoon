Cattery or cat sitter? What's actually better for your cat

19 September 2026 · Purr & Home, Chorley

Both options keep your cat fed, safe and looked after while you're away — the difference is what the experience is actually like for your cat, not just the logistics for you.

The case for a cattery

A good cattery is fully staffed around the clock, which matters if your cat needs frequent monitoring, or if you're away for a long stretch and want round-the-clock cover rather than daily visits. It's a straightforward option if you're booking well in advance.

The case for a cat sitter

Most cats are territorial by nature, and a change of environment — new smells, new sounds, other cats nearby — is often more unsettling than being left alone in familiar surroundings. A sitter who visits at home means:

  • No carrier, no car journey, no waiting room.
  • Your cat keeps their own routine, litter tray and favourite spots.
  • One familiar person, rather than rotating cattery staff.
  • Visits timed around your cat, not a cattery's opening hours.

What tends to suit which cat

Confident, sociable cats who don't mind change often do fine either way. Nervous cats, older cats, or those on a strict medical routine usually settle better at home, where nothing around them has changed except a familiar face popping in.

If you're weighing it up for a trip from Chorley, it's worth booking a free meet & greet either way — it's the easiest way to judge how your own cat is likely to react.
