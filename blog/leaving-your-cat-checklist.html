What to leave out for your cat sitter: a quick checklist

19 September 2026 · Purr & Home, Chorley

A good first visit runs smoothly because everything a sitter needs is easy to find — not because they're guessing where the food is kept. Here's what's worth setting out before you go.

Food and feeding instructions

Leave enough food for the whole trip, plus a little extra in case plans change. Write down exact portions and times if your cat is on a schedule, and flag anything they're not allowed (some cats are on prescription or sensitive diets).

Litter tray supplies

Spare litter, a scoop, and bin bags for the waste all in one place saves a sitter hunting under the sink. If you use tray liners, leave a few spare.

Medication, clearly labelled

If your cat takes medication, leave it with the dosage and timing written down, along with your vet's contact details. It's worth mentioning this at the meet & greet too, so nothing gets missed on a first visit.

Hiding spots and habits

Nervous cats often have a favourite hiding place when someone new comes in. Letting your sitter know where to expect them saves a stressful search, and means they know your cat is fine even if they don't appear straight away.

Emergency details

Your vet's name and number, a neighbour or friend who could help in a pinch, and the best way to reach you while you're away. It's rarely needed, but it makes a real difference on the day it is.

A spare key, somewhere sensible

If you're handing over a key at a meet & greet, that's usually all that's needed. If you'd rather leave one hidden or with a neighbour, just make sure your sitter knows exactly where before the first visit, not on the day.

None of this takes long to put together, and it means the very first visit goes as smoothly as the tenth. If you're booking a cat sitter in Chorley for the first time, this is exactly the sort of thing we run through at the free meet & greet.
